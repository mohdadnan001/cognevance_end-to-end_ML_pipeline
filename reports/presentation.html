The clinical problem

Why it matters

  • Readmissions signal gaps in discharge planning and follow-up care.
  • They consume bed capacity and increase cost of care.
  • Early risk flags can prioritize transitional-care resources.

Project objective

  • Build a reproducible, leak-free predictive pipeline.
  • Compare classical ML and deep learning under class imbalance.
  • Deploy the best model as an API with an interactive dashboard.

Dataset

Diabetes 130-US Hospitals, 1999–2008

At a glance

  • 101,766 encounters · 71,518 unique patients
  • 50 raw columns: demographics, utilization, labs, 23 medications, ICD-9 codes
  • Target: readmitted < 30 days → positive class
  • 11,357 positives vs 90,409 negatives
  • Public, de-identified, CC BY 4.0 (UCI DOI 10.24432/C5230J)

1.42 encounters per patient — repeated patients require group-aware splitting.

Decisions

  • Drop weight and payer_code; keep specialty as Unknown.
  • Preserve None in lab results — it means “not measured”.
  • Map ? to Unknown; median-impute numerics.
  • One-hot encode categoricals with rare-category grouping (min frequency 20).
  • Group ICD-9 diagnosis codes into nine clinical categories.

Split & tuning

  • 7-fold StratifiedGroupKFold grouped on patient_nbr.
  • No patient appears in more than one split (asserted).
  • Threshold tuned on validation to maximize F1; test scored once.
  • PR-AUC is the primary metric due to 11.16% prevalence.

Model zoo

  • Logistic regression (balanced)
  • Random forest (200 trees)
  • Histogram gradient boosting
  • XGBoost (scale_pos_weight, aucpr)
  • TensorFlow/Keras MLP: 128 → 64 → 1

Reading the scorecard

  • PR-AUC 0.2345 vs 0.1116 baseline = 2.1× lift.
  • Recall-first operating point: catches ~48% of readmissions.
  • Precision ~21%: alerts need clinical review, not automatic action.
  • Brier score 0.208 — calibration can be improved.

Decision policy

The threshold is a clinical capacity decision

At threshold 0.53

  • Recall 47.8%, precision 20.6%, F1 0.288.
  • Lower threshold → more alerts, higher recall, more false positives.
  • Higher threshold → fewer alerts, lower recall.
  • Capacity-constrained programs should optimize cost per avoided readmission, not accuracy.

Interpretability

Prior utilization dominates the signal

Permutation importance

  • Prior inpatient visits and inpatient ratio are the strongest drivers.
  • Discharge disposition captures post-discharge setting and severity.
  • Diagnosis count and length of stay proxy clinical complexity.
  • Importance is predictive, not causal — no intervention claims.

Responsible use

Strong baseline — not a clinical device

Validation

Temporal drift

Random split on 1999–2008 data may overestimate performance on current patients. Temporal and external validation are required.

Fairness

Subgroup audit

Race, gender and age are features; no fairness audit yet. Risk models can amplify inequities if deployed without safeguards.

Imbalance

Alert burden

At 48% recall, precision is ~21%. Alerts must support — not replace — clinical judgment.

Scope

Not medical advice

Research and educational use only. No causal claims, no regulatory clearance, no PHI.
